# Table. Evidence Supporting Scenarios — Urogynecology (URPS) Workforce Projection

Modeled after Fraher et al., *Forecasting the Future Supply of Pediatric Subspecialists* (Pediatrics 2024;153[S2]:e2023063678C), Table 5. Each scenario is run through the same age-structured projection engine (`wc_project`, horizon 4) so that a change reflects the stated input, not the model. The primary specification is marked **(primary)**.

| Scenario | Implementation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Baseline workforce estimand** | (1) 2023 board-certified active URPS = **1,306** national **(primary)**; (2) 2025 roster snapshot = **1,339**; (3) published legacy cohort = **1,295** (frozen; reproduced from the record, not recalculated) | Released mufflyaccess workforce artifact, contract v3.0.0 (ABOG subspecialty-certification basis; ABU urology-pathway net-new deduplicated against the ABOG National Provider Identifier set). The frozen 1,295 reproduces the published Society of Gynecologic Surgeons (SGS) projection. |
| **Increase and decrease in fellowship output** | (4) Status-quo **64** completions/y **(primary)**; (5) increase to **74**/y; (6) decrease to **61**/y (recent-low contraction); (7) cohort-accounting **66**/y | ACGME accredited URPS fellowship completions, mean of academic years 2020–2024 (61, 66, 63, 66). The optimistic case uses the National Resident Matching Program (NRMP) certified-position benchmark (74). |
| **Graduate entry into practice** | (8) 100% enter active U.S. practice immediately **(primary)**; (9) 85% enter; (10) 70% conversion floor; (11) transition-ramped entry (graduates build practice over ~5 y) | Not all fellowship graduates enter active clinical practice immediately (relocation, non-clinical roles, delayed practice). The ramped curve defers early-career build-up and reproduces the manuscript's transition-adjusted projection. |
| **Increase in departure rate** | (12) Empirical age-band departure hazard **×1.0 (primary)**; (13) **×1.5**; (14) **×2.0** (stress); departure defined at a 2-, 3-, or 4-year clinical-inactivity threshold | Empirically estimated pooled gynecologic-oncology + URPS age-band departure hazards from events/person-years, 2016–2021 (35 URPS departures over 6,403 person-years). Stakeholder concern that observed departures understate future attrition. |
| **Hazard estimation method** | (15) Partially pooled GO + URPS hierarchical hazard **(primary)**; (16) fully pooled; (17) unpooled subspecialty-specific | Sparse per-band events make unpooled subspecialty hazards unstable; a hierarchical (partial-pooling) model borrows strength across the two board-certified subspecialties (unpooled ratio 4.83 [2.79–6.1] vs pooled 5.61 [4.07–6.71]). |
| **Death and departure under-ascertainment** | (18) No mortality adjustment **(primary)**; (19) add half of expected missed deaths; (20) add all expected missed deaths | Classified deaths are near zero in the administrative departure signals (under-ascertainment). Expected mortality (≈ 7.8 URPS deaths/y from age- and sex-specific life tables) is added to observed departures as a conservative bound. |
| **ABU urology-pathway departure** | (21) ABU departures = ABOG age-band hazard **(primary)**; (22) ABU hazard **×0.5**; (23) ABU hazard **×2.0**; (24) ABOG-only cohort (1,031; excludes the urology pathway) | No ABU-pathway-specific departure data exist; because urology-pathway urogynecologists are certified and deduplicated but not separately observed for exit, their departure risk is bounded around the ABOG estimate. |
| **Combination scenarios** | (25) "Best case": +12.5% fellows, 100% entry, ×0.5 ABU hazard; (26) "Worst case": −graduate contraction (61/y), 70% entry, ×2.0 departure, all missed deaths | The evidence for each contributing scenario is detailed above. The combinations bound plausible best- and worst-case futures in which several factors move the available workforce together. |

*ABOG, American Board of Obstetrics and Gynecology; ABU, American Board of Urology; ACGME, Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education; GO, gynecologic oncology; NRMP, National Resident Matching Program; SGS, Society of Gynecologic Surgeons; URPS, urogynecology and reconstructive pelvic surgery.*

*Every scenario is projected on the same age-structured engine (`wc_project`, horizon 4, 64 entrants/y at age 34) with a Binomial Monte Carlo 95% interval (fixed seed). Sources: `data/graduate_growth_scenarios.csv`, `data/mortality_sensitivity.csv`, `data/inactivity_threshold_sensitivity.csv`, `data/hierarchical_hazard_comparison.csv`, `data/abu_pathway_sensitivity.csv`, and the contract-v3.0.0 baseline scenarios.*
